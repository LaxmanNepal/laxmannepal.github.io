## Editorial rule

**Do not publish an unverified claim just because it appeared in an earlier briefing.** Several historical briefs contain claims about very recent products and launches that were not independently confirmed in this archive. Treat them as research leads, not established facts. Verify with official product documentation, release notes, pricing/availability pages, and credible reporting. If the claim cannot be confirmed, drop it or clearly label it as unconfirmed.

## Suggested daily operating loop

1. Find 8–12 candidate stories from the last 24–72 hours.
2. Verify the primary source, date, geography, rollout status, and limitations.
3. Score each idea for freshness, audience fit, searchable intent, demo potential, and credibility.
4. Select 3 Shorts + 1 long-form idea per channel.
5. Create separate packaging for each audience; do not merely translate one video.
6. Record the screen/demo before writing the final script.
7. Publish, then log impressions, CTR, retention, views, subscribers gained, and production time after 24 hours and 7 days.

## Priority scoring

Score each candidate from 1–5:
- **Freshness:** is it genuinely new or newly relevant?
- **Audience fit:** does it solve a problem for this channel's audience?
- **Search intent:** would a viewer actively search for this?
- **Demo potential:** can Laxman show a real workflow or test?
- **Source confidence:** is the claim supported by reliable, preferably primary, sources?

Prioritize high total scores, but reject ideas with low source confidence regardless of score.
