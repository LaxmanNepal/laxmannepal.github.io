# Daily Topic Research and Verification Workflow

## 1. Search fresh, but do not trust search snippets

Look for developments from the last 24–72 hours, then open the source. Prioritize:
1. Official release notes, product blogs, support pages, model cards, regulator documents.
2. Reputable reporting with dates and named sources.
3. Developer documentation and reproducible demonstrations.
4. Social posts only as leads, not final evidence.

Useful source families:
- YouTube Official Blog and YouTube Help
- Google Blog, Google Workspace Updates, Gemini Help, Google AI for Developers
- Microsoft official blogs, Windows Insider Blog, Microsoft Learn
- Meta/Instagram official newsroom and product documentation
- OpenAI News and Help Center
- Official Nepal Rastra Bank, NCHL, wallet/provider help pages, Nepal Telecom, relevant ministries and event organizers
- Reuters and established technology publications for independent reporting

## 2. Verify six things before writing

For every time-sensitive idea, record:
- **What happened?** Exact claim, not a vague paraphrase.
- **When?** Announcement date vs release date vs date the story was reported.
- **Where?** Global, U.S.-only, selected countries, or limited preview.
- **Who qualifies?** Age, account type, subscription, device, OS, or organization.
- **What is the source?** Link and publication/update date.
- **What is still uncertain?** Clearly note unknowns or conflicting statements.

Never confuse “announced,” “in testing,” “rolling out,” and “generally available.”

## 3. Build a separate audience angle

### Nepali channel
Ask:
- Can a Nepali viewer access this now?
- Is it free or affordable?
- Does it work on Android or an older PC?
- Is there a Nepal-specific payment, language, device, education, or office use case?
- Can the whole workflow be demonstrated in Nepali?

### U.S. channel
Ask:
- Does it affect a U.S. creator, worker, developer, or consumer?
- Can we compare it against a familiar alternative?
- Can we measure cost, time saved, output quality, reliability, privacy, or permissions?
- Does the story have a useful decision: use it, skip it, wait, or change workflow?

Do not just translate titles. The viewer problem and video structure should differ.

## 4. Score ideas

Rate each dimension 1–5:
- Freshness
- Search intent
- Audience fit
- Demonstration potential
- Source confidence

A topic with low source confidence is **not publish-ready**, even if it is trending. Use high-confidence primary evidence for claims about price, rollout, model capability, safety incidents, and eligibility.

## 5. A repeatable Short structure

1. **0–2 seconds:** Show the outcome or surprising limitation.
2. **2–8 seconds:** State the real problem.
3. **8–25 seconds:** Demonstrate the workflow.
4. **25–40 seconds:** Show failure, caveat, cost, or limitation.
5. **Final beat:** Clear verdict and a relevant follow-up question.

Avoid long intros, unsupported superlatives, and claims like “best ever” without a test.

## 6. Long-form structure

- State the viewer's problem and promise.
- Show the setup and conditions.
- Run the same tasks across tools or scenarios.
- Track time, cost, quality, errors, corrections, privacy, and reliability.
- Show failures as well as successes.
- Give a verdict by user type.
- Put sources and test conditions in the description.

## 7. After publishing

Log at 24 hours and 7 days:
- Impressions
- Click-through rate (long-form)
- Viewed vs swiped away (Shorts)
- Average view duration / percentage viewed
- Retention dips
- Views and traffic sources
- Subscribers gained
- Comments/questions
- Production time
- Whether the video generated a follow-up idea

Compare each video against the channel's own recent baseline, not another channel or a generic benchmark. Keep topic, title, thumbnail, and outcome data together so the next briefing improves over time.

## 8. Daily briefing output template

For each channel, provide 3 Shorts and 1 long-form:
- Strong title
- First-line hook
- Why timely/searchable
- Demonstration angle
- Keywords
- Source links and verification status
- Availability caveat
- Suggested publishing priority

End with one clear recommendation for what to record first. The goal is not to produce more ideas; it is to help the creator choose and publish the best evidence-backed idea.
